Square-Bore A4 Flute

A five-hole minor-pentatonic flute in A4, in the Native American style, rebuilt in 3mm ply with the bore area of a 7/8in round-bore flute: 387mm², the same air column in a section a laser can build. Six variants from one acoustic model, the same six again with the ideas of the 2017 flute folded in, and that flute itself, redrawn at A4.

Bore shape barely matters

A 19.67mm square and an 18.43 × 21mm rectangle of the same area put every hole within 0.1mm of each other. Box or stack is a building choice, not an acoustic one.

Wall thickness matters most

Moving the holes from 3mm of wall to 6mm moves every one of them 10–16mm toward the window, because a hole's chimney is part of its length.

Lay it out for the hands

Equal holes land 27, 30, 46 and 12mm apart. Holes 3 + 2, 25mm apart within each hand, with each hole sized to its note, puts the gap between the hands where it belongs.

In the first six the flue is engraved in the bird's underside, shown blue; in the -plus variants and the 2017 flute it is engraved in the top plate over the plug, where the bird covers it.

All twelve variants

Every variant carries the 2017 flute's mouthpiece cap, with its half-round notch in the bottom edge. Each -plus variant is the one above it with three more of that flute's ideas: a window and an exit hole the whole width of the bore, the flue engraved in the nest rather than the bird, and a soft depression round every finger hole. The wider window has the smaller end correction, so the model puts the foot 16.5–18mm further down. The 2017 flute's engraved splitting edge is not among them, since it charred, and nor is its stepped block, which was for looks only.

A laminated square bore is missing on purpose. A stack's depth is a whole number of 3mm layers, 18 or 21mm, and 19.67 is neither, so the stack keeps the area and gives up the square.

Building any of them

  1. Flue. The bottom bird layer carries the flue as a 10mm-wide fill engrave. Set its depth to about 1mm on scrap first; it is the one dimension here that is a power setting rather than a cut.
  2. Splitting edge. File the window's downstream edge to a bevel of about 30°, before assembly, while the top is still a flat plate. If it goes wrong, recut the top.
  3. Assemble. Box: press-fit, then shellac every seam. Stack: glue the layers in order against a straightedge. The doubler and the bird are glued in both.
  4. Tune the fundamental. With every hole closed, trim the foot until it sounds A4.
  5. Tune the holes. Work up from hole 1, opening each hole slightly with a file if it is flat. Each hole nudges the ones above it.

The window's end correction is the uncalibrated term: each 1mm of it moves the foot about 4.5mm, or 2.6mm with a full-width window. That is why the foot is cut long. The holes move with it almost as far, 2.3–3.0mm for the foot's 3.5mm, so trimming the foot does not tune them: each is tuned in turn, and only a flat hole can be fixed. The first build to be cut and played will set the term for the rest. Flutopedia's bore table also puts A4 nearer a 3/4in bore than 7/8in, so this reference is on the large side for its key.

The author's flute

Designed, cut and played in 2017, and kept here as it was in yours/original/. Its bore is 19.37 × 22.44mm, 435mm², and its window runs the whole width of the bore. It tried an engraved splitting edge, a greyscale ramp beside the window meant to let the laser cut the bevel; that did not work, so the edge is filed by hand here as in every variant above. Each finger hole also sits in a soft engraved depression for the finger.

Scaled to A4 and redrawn as yours-flat-A4 and yours-fingers-A4 in the 3D view. Every dimension that sets the pitch is multiplied by 1.0705, solved so the model gives 440Hz: the bore, now 20.74 × 24.02mm, the window, the holes' positions and the sound chamber. The 3mm wall cannot scale, so the hole diameters are re-solved instead, until every note lands on equal temperament; holes 2 and 3 come out about 1mm smaller than scaling would make them. The builds at the original size were removed at the author's asking. Millimetre-true, kerf-compensated, in the house colours and numbered; the plug made four 3mm discs, 12mm, by moving the exit hole 1mm toward the mouth; the ramp left out and the edge filed by hand; and in the fingers build the top finger-jointed like the bottom, so it press-fits.
