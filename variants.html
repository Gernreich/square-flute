Square-Bore A4 Flute

A five-hole minor-pentatonic flute in A4, in the Native American style, rebuilt in 3mm ply with the bore area of a 7/8in round-bore flute: 387mm², the same air column in a section a laser can build. Six variants, from one acoustic model and one generator.

Bore shape barely matters

A 19.67mm square and an 18.43 × 21mm rectangle of the same area put every hole within 0.1mm of each other. Box or stack is a building choice, not an acoustic one.

Wall thickness matters most

Moving the holes from 3mm of wall to 6mm moves every one of them 10–16mm toward the window, because a hole's chimney is part of its length.

Lay it out for the hands

Equal holes land 27, 30, 46 and 12mm apart. Holes 3 + 2, 25mm apart within each hand, with each hole sized to its note, puts the gap between the hands where it belongs.

A laminated square bore is missing on purpose. A stack's depth is a whole number of 3mm layers, 18 or 21mm, and 19.67 is neither, so the stack keeps the area and gives up the square.

Building any of them

  1. Flue. The bottom bird layer carries the flue as a 10mm-wide fill engrave. Set its depth to about 1mm on scrap first; it is the one dimension here that is a power setting rather than a cut.
  2. Splitting edge. File the window's downstream edge to a bevel of about 30°. The top is cut as a short head plate and a long body plate, so a spoilt edge costs the short one.
  3. Assemble. Box: press-fit, then shellac every seam. Stack: glue the layers in order against a straightedge. The doubler and the bird are glued in both.
  4. Tune the fundamental. With every hole closed, trim the foot until it sounds A4.
  5. Tune the holes. Work up from hole 1, opening each hole slightly with a file if it is flat. Each hole nudges the ones above it.

The window's end correction is the uncalibrated term: each 1mm of it moves the foot about 5.5mm. That is why the foot is cut long. Measure one built flute and the model can be set to it. Flutopedia's bore table also puts A4 nearer a 3/4in bore than 7/8in, so this reference is on the large side for its key.

The author's flute

Designed, cut and played in 2017, and kept here as it was in yours/original/. Its bore is 19.37 × 22.44mm, 435mm², and its window runs the whole width of the bore. It tried an engraved splitting edge, a greyscale ramp beside the window meant to let the laser cut the bevel; that did not work, so the edge is filed by hand here as in the six above. Each finger hole also sits in a soft engraved depression for the finger.

Redrawn as yours-flat and yours-fingers in the 3D view, with the window, every hole and the foot where they were. Millimetre-true, kerf-compensated, in the house colours and numbered; the plug made four 3mm discs, 12mm, by moving the exit hole 1mm toward the mouth; the ramp left out and the top cut as a head plate and a body plate, so a spoilt edge costs the short one; and in the fingers build the top box-jointed like the bottom, so it press-fits.
