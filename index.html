Square-bore flute

See every variant, in 3D · Read the writeup

A five-hole minor-pentatonic flute in A4, in the Native American style, rebuilt in 3mm plywood with a square or rectangular bore. It keeps the bore area of a 7/8in (22.2mm) round-bore flute, 387mm², so the air column is the same in a section a laser can build. All closed it sounds A4; opening the holes from the foot up gives C5, D5, E5, G5 and A5.

Output is millimetre-true — 1 user unit = 1 mm with a physical width/height — so it prints and cuts at real size.

None of these variants has been cut or played yet. The holes and the foot come from an acoustic model, and the foot is cut long to be trimmed to pitch. The author's own flute of 2017, which has been, is here too, with six more variants that borrow its ideas.

The six variants

Three ways to build the bore, each with the finger holes in 3mm or 6mm of wall:

Variant Construction Bore Hole wall Parts Sheets Joints
box-square-3 finger-jointed box 19.67 × 19.67mm 3mm 13 1 the bird and the cap glued, the rest press-fit
box-square-6 finger-jointed box 19.67 × 19.67mm 6mm 14 1 the doubler, the bird and the cap glued
box-rect-3 finger-jointed box 18.43 × 21mm 3mm 13 1 the bird and the cap glued, the rest press-fit
box-rect-6 finger-jointed box 18.43 × 21mm 6mm 14 1 the doubler, the bird and the cap glued
stack-rect-3 laminated stack 18.43 × 21mm 3mm 14 2 every layer and the cap glued
stack-rect-6 laminated stack 18.43 × 21mm 6mm 15 2 every layer and the cap glued
  • The box is four plates: the two sides are tabbed into the top and the bottom, and the plug is four discs pressed into the bore.
  • The stack is a bottom layer, seven bore layers with the bore cut out of them and the plug left solid, and the top.
  • The 6mm variants glue a 3mm doubler over the run of finger holes, so each hole has a 6mm chimney, as a wooden flute's wall would give it.

Every variant carries the author's mouthpiece cap from the 2017 flute below: a plate glued over the mouth end, with a half-round notch in its bottom edge, away from the finger holes. The notch is a 9.59mm circle centred on the edge, and it opens through the bottom wall into the slow air chamber.

There is no laminated square bore. A stack is a whole number of 3mm layers deep, 18 or 21mm, and 19.67 is neither, so the stack keeps the area and gives up the square. box-rect is the stack's bore built as a box, so the two constructions can be compared at one section.

Each of the six is about 469mm long, and each -plus variant about 486mm. All of them fit a 600 × 308mm bed.

What the model says

The bore's shape barely matters. The square and the 18.43 × 21mm rectangle put every hole within 0.1mm of each other. Box or stack is a building choice, not an acoustic one.

The wall's thickness matters most. With the holes in 6mm of wall instead of 3mm, every one of them sits 10–16mm nearer the window, because a hole's chimney is part of its length.

The holes are laid out for the hands. Five equal 8mm holes land 27, 30, 46 and 12mm apart, and two fingers will not cover holes 12mm apart. So the lower hand takes holes 1–3 and the upper hand 4–5, each hand's holes 25mm apart, and each hole is sized to its note:

Holes in Sound chamber Hole 1, C5 Hole 2, D5 Hole 3, E5 Hole 4, G5 Hole 5, A5
3mm of wall 328.9mm 229.7 · ø7.7 204.7 · ø8.0 179.7 · ø9.2 129.4 · ø8.0 104.4 · ø6.2
6mm of wall 327.2mm 219.2 · ø8.1 194.2 · ø8.0 169.2 · ø8.7 113.7 · ø8.0 88.7 · ø6.8

Distances are from the centre of the window, in mm. Hole 1 is nearest the foot.

The scale as ratios

Each note's frequency against the lowest one, as the just ratio that names the interval, and the step up from the note below it:

Note Interval from A4 Ratio from A4 Step from the note below Just, cents Tuned to, cents
A4 unison 1:1 0 0
C5 minor third 6:5 6:5, a minor third 315.6 300
D5 perfect fourth 4:3 10:9, a minor whole tone 498.0 500
E5 perfect fifth 3:2 9:8, a major whole tone 702.0 700
G5 minor seventh 9:5 6:5, a minor third 1017.6 1000
A5 octave 2:1 10:9, a minor whole tone 1200.0 1200

The steps run 6:5, 10:9, 9:8, 6:5, 10:9: two minor thirds and three whole tones, and no semitone anywhere, which is why any of its notes sound well together. The model tunes to equal temperament, the last column, so every flute here is designed within 18 cents of the just ratios: the minor third and the minor seventh sit 15.6 and 17.6 cents flat of 6:5 and 9:5, and the fourth and the fifth are 2 cents off. The minor seventh has two other common just values: 16:9 at 996.1 cents and the harmonic 7:4 at 968.8.

Before you cut

The window's end correction is not measured. It is the least certain term in the model, and no built flute has checked it yet. Each 1mm of error in it moves the foot about 4.5mm, or 2.6mm with a full-width window. So the sound chamber is cut 15mm long: trim the foot until the flute sounds A4 with every hole closed.

The holes move with it, almost as far. A 10% error in that correction moves the foot 3.5mm and every hole 2.3–3.0mm. Trimming the foot puts the fundamental right but leaves the holes where the model put them, so expect to tune each one. A hole that plays flat can be opened with a file. A hole that plays sharp cannot be closed up.

7/8in is a large bore for A4. Flutopedia's bore table puts A4 nearer 3/4in. This is the bore that was asked for, but most flutes in this key are narrower.

The flue is a fill engrave, about 1mm deep. In the six variants it is 10mm wide, on the underside of the bottom bird layer. In the -plus variants and the 2017 flute, it is on the top plate over the plug, the full width of the bore. The depth is a power setting, so find it on scrap first.

File the splitting edge by hand. The window's far edge, where the air splits, needs a clean bevel of about 30°, and a laser cuts square, charred edges. The top is cut as a short head plate and a long body plate, so a spoilt edge costs only the short one.

Cut it in 3mm Baltic birch plywood, at a 0.13mm kerf. The tabs are drawn 0.19mm tight along their length and 0.12mm across the ply, the mallet fit used on the lyre-harp. Press-fit, then seal every seam with shellac. The doubler, the bird's layers, every layer of the stack and every mouthpiece cap are glued.

Run the colours blue, orange, black: blue engraves the part numbers and the flue, orange cuts the holes, slots and windows, and black frees the parts. That is the sequence every LaserMadeMusic repository uses. The -plus variants and the 2017 flute also carry a greyscale image round each finger hole, the depression. It needs an image engrave that turns grey into depth, such as Grayscale mode in xTool Creative Space.

Tuning

  1. Tie the bird on over the exit hole, its front edge on the window's near edge. It is tied rather than glued, so it can be slid to voice the flute.
  2. With every hole closed, trim the foot until the flute sounds A4.
  3. Open hole 1 and tune C5. The holes are cut to the model's size, so if a note is flat, open its hole slightly with a file.
  4. Work up the flute in order, since each hole nudges the ones above it.

Regenerating it

python3 naf_model.py                          # the holes for every variant, printed
python3 naf_model.py --mouth=0.8              # with a different window correction
python3 square_flute.py --no-write            # the generator's checks, writing nothing
python3 square_flute.py --out=cut-files       # every variant into cut-files/<variant>/
python3 plus_flute.py --out=cut-files         # the -plus variants
python3 your_flute.py --out=yours/cut-files   # the 2017 flute, redrawn
python3 compare_page.py --standalone variants.html

naf_model.py is a transmission-matrix model, the standard way woodwind calculators predict pitch. It treats the bore as a lossy tube, using the section's area and its hydraulic radius, which is where a square differs from a circle. Each finger hole is Keefe's tone hole, open or closed. At the top, the window opens into the bore beside a short closed stub up to the plug.

square_flute.py builds each variant from what the model asks for and checks it before writing anything:

  • every note within a cent of its target
  • every part on the bed
  • every hole inside the bore
  • enough ply between neighbouring holes, and between a hole and a mortice
  • the joint in the top clear of the window and the top hole
  • every part placed on a sheet

It borrows the stroke font for its part numbers from ribbon_bore.py in trumpet-elbows-allowed, so it expects that repository cloned beside this one.

.repro holds the exact command for every file here.

The -plus variants

Each of the six again, with three more ideas from the author's flute below, beyond the mouthpiece cap that every variant has:

  1. A window the whole width of the bore, rather than 10mm of it.
  2. An exit hole the whole width too, with the flue engraved in the nest, over the plug, rather than in the bird. The bird is then three plain layers.
  3. A soft engraved depression round every finger hole, the original's own image, 1.57 times the hole across. On a 6mm variant it is on the doubler, where the finger lands.

A wider window has a smaller end correction, so the model puts the foot 16.5–18mm further down, and every hole moves with it:

Variant Sound chamber Hole 1 Hole 2 Hole 3 Hole 4 Hole 5
box-square-3-plus 346.9mm 245.9 · ø7.6 220.9 · ø8.0 195.9 · ø9.4 143.0 · ø8.0 118.0 · ø6.4
box-square-6-plus 345.2mm 235.3 · ø8.0 210.3 · ø8.0 185.3 · ø8.9 126.4 · ø8.0 101.4 · ø7.0
box-rect-3-plus, stack-rect-3-plus 345.4mm 244.5 · ø7.6 219.5 · ø8.0 194.5 · ø9.4 141.8 · ø8.0 116.8 · ø6.4
box-rect-6-plus, stack-rect-6-plus 343.7mm 233.9 · ø8.1 208.9 · ø8.0 183.9 · ø8.9 125.3 · ø8.0 100.3 · ø7.0

Here the square and the rectangle differ by 1.5mm, where the six agree to 0.1. That is the window, not the bore: it is as wide as the bore, so the narrower rectangle has the narrower window.

In the box, a full-width opening reaches the inner faces of the bore, which is where the mortices are, so no tab stands beside the exit hole or the window. The cap and the bird's layers are glued, as in the original. Two things are not taken. The engraved splitting edge charred, so the edge is still filed by hand and the top still split. And the original's stepped block was for looks only, so the bird keeps its plain layers.

The author's flute

The six variants above are a fresh take, worked out without seeing this one. This flute was designed, cut and played in 2017, and its files are in yours/original/ exactly as they were. Measured out of yours/original/NAF_flatjoints.svg:

Bore 19.37 wide × 22.44mm deep, 435mm²
Sound chamber 313.25mm from the window's centre to the foot
Window 19.37 × 6.0mm, the whole width of the bore
Plug 11mm, its top engraved as the flue
Finger holes 7.73, 7.87, 7.79, 7.79 and 5.47mm, 213.9, 185.7, 156.9, 109.5 and 78.0mm from the window

It tried two things the six variants do not:

  • An engraved splitting edge. FluteEdge.png is a greyscale ramp, black at the window and white 9.9mm on, engraved beside the window so the laser would cut the bevel itself. It did not work, so the edge is filed by hand, as it is in the six variants.
  • A soft engraved depression round each finger hole, ring.png, for the finger to find. These worked, cut on a Glowforge, whose 3D engrave reads a grey image as depth. On an xTool P2S, Grayscale mode in xTool Creative Space varies the power with each pixel's brightness, so the same image should work there too. Set its depth on scrap first.
FluteEdge.png · the bevel, which did not work ring.png · the depression

Its flue is the six variants' idea on the other face: an engrave over the plug, in the nest rather than in the bird.

Redrawn

your_flute.py redraws it in two builds:

  • yours-flat: the sides butted between the top and the bottom, and glued.
  • yours-fingers: box-jointed top and bottom.

The window, every finger hole and the foot stay exactly where they were. The tweaks:

  1. Millimetre-true, in the house cut-order colours, kerf-compensated, with every part numbered. The originals are drawn in Inkscape's 1/90in units with no kerf.
  2. The plug is four 3mm discs, 12mm instead of 11. The exit hole moves 1mm toward the mouth to make room.
  3. In the fingers build the top is box-jointed to the sides, like the bottom, so it press-fits instead of being glued.
  4. The rings are the original's own image, embedded at its original size. The ramp is left out, because the engraved bevel did not work.
  5. With the edge filed by hand, the top is cut as a short head plate and a long body plate, butted 20mm past the window, as in the six variants. A spoilt edge then costs only the short plate.

Nothing acoustic is changed. The mouthpiece cap and the block's layers are glued, as they were. The leather tie is its own file: cut it from leather, not ply.

The shape of the scale agrees to within 40 cents at every hole, which is the best test the model has had. Its pitch, near B♭4, rests on the same uncalibrated window term as everything else here. The 2017 flutes have all been given away, so that term cannot be measured from one. The first of these builds to be cut and played will set it, and with it the foot of every build here.
