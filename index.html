Square-bore flute

← All projects

Read the README · Turn it in 3D on a page of its own

A five-hole minor-pentatonic flute in A4, in the Native American style, rebuilt in 3 mm plywood with a square or rectangular bore. It keeps the bore area of a 3/4in (19.05 mm) round-bore flute, 285mm², so the air column is the same in a section a laser can build. All closed it sounds A4; opening the holes from the foot up gives C5, D5, E5, G5 and A5.

Output is millimetre-true — 1 user unit = 1mm with a physical width/height — so it prints and cuts at real size.

None of these builds has been cut or played yet. The holes and the foot come from an acoustic model, and the foot is cut long to be trimmed to pitch. The author's own flute of 2017, which has been, is described here too, and most of its ideas are in every build.

The builds

Eight builds: six in A4, and two long ones in F4 and E4 (see The long flutes). The box and the stack each come with the finger holes in 3 mm or 6 mm of wall, and the boxes come finger-jointed or flat, butt-jointed and glued as the 2017 flute was: box-square-3-flat. The 3D view below has a toggle for each. Every build carries its flue both ways at once, in the bird and in the nest, each in a colour of its own, so the laser cuts whichever you choose: see Bird, nest or savanna flue.

  • The box is four plates, finger-jointed edge to edge: the sides' fingers fill notches in the edges of the top and the bottom and run flush to the outside faces, so no plate is left outboard of a joint. The plug is four discs pressed into the bore. A -flat box has plain plates instead, the sides glued between the top and the bottom: the same parts, the same count, and glue in place of a press-fit.
  • The stack is a bottom layer, six bore layers with the bore cut out of them and the plug left solid, and the top.
  • The 6 mm builds glue a 3 mm doubler over the run of finger holes, so each hole has a 6 mm chimney, as a wooden flute's wall would give it.

Every build carries the author's mouthpiece cap from the 2017 flute below: a plate glued over the mouth end, with a half-round notch in its bottom edge, away from the finger holes. The notch is a 9.59 mm circle centred 0.33 mm inside the edge, 5.1 mm deep, so it opens through the bottom wall into the slow air chamber. The bottom plate has a second half-round notch, the same 9.59 mm circle, cut into its mouth end midway across. It is at right angles to the cap's, so the two meet at the bottom corner as one rounded opening into the slow air chamber.

Every build has the 2017 flute's window and exit hole too: both run the whole width of the bore, with the flue joining them across that same width. And every finger hole sits in the 2017 flute's soft engraved depression, its ring.png kept in the original's proportion to the hole, 1.57 times it across; on a 6 mm build the depressions are on the doubler, where the finger lands. In the box no finger stands beside either opening, since a full-width opening reaches the inner face of the bore, which is the floor of every finger notch.

The box is square, the bore that was asked for. The stack cannot be: it is a whole number of 3 mm layers deep, 15 or 18 mm, and 16.88 is neither, so it keeps the area and gives up the square.

Every build is 491–495 mm long, and all of them fit a 600 × 305 mm bed.

What the model says

The bore's shape barely matters. The square and the 15.83 × 18 mm rectangle put every hole within 1.2 mm of each other, and even that is the window rather than the bore: the window is as wide as the bore, so the narrower rectangle has the narrower window. At one window width the two agree to 0.1 mm. Box or stack is a building choice, not an acoustic one.

The wall's thickness matters most. With the holes in 6 mm of wall instead of 3 mm, every one of them sits 8.6–10.1 mm nearer the window, because a hole's chimney is part of its length.

The holes are laid out for the hands. Five equal 8 mm holes land 30, 31, 45 and 18 mm apart in 3 mm of wall, and 28, 30, 47 and 15 mm apart in 6 mm; two fingers will not cover holes 15 or 18 mm apart. So the lower hand takes holes 1–3 and the upper hand 4–5, each hand's holes 25 mm apart, and each hole is sized to its note. A flat box shares its jointed twin's layout:

The scale as ratios

Each note's frequency against the lowest one, as the just ratio that names the interval, and the step up from the note below it:

Note Interval from A4 Ratio from A4 Step from the note below Just, cents Tuned to, cents
A4 unison 1:1 0 0
C5 minor third 6:5 6:5, a minor third 315.6 300
D5 perfect fourth 4:3 10:9, a minor whole tone 498.0 500
E5 perfect fifth 3:2 9:8, a major whole tone 702.0 700
G5 minor seventh 9:5 6:5, a minor third 1017.6 1000
A5 octave 2:1 10:9, a minor whole tone 1200.0 1200

The steps run 6:5, 10:9, 9:8, 6:5, 10:9: two minor thirds and three whole tones, and no semitone anywhere: no two notes are a half step apart. The model tunes to equal temperament, the last column, so every flute here is designed within 18 cents of the just ratios: the minor third and the minor seventh sit 15.6 and 17.6 cents flat of 6:5 and 9:5, and the fourth and the fifth are 2 cents off. The minor seventh has two other common just values: 16:9 at 996.1 cents and the harmonic 7:4 at 968.8.

Before you cut

The window's end correction is not measured. It is the least certain term in the model, and no built flute has checked it yet. Each 1 mm of error in it moves the foot about 2.3 mm. So the sound chamber is cut 15 mm longer than the model's: trim the foot until the flute sounds A4 with every hole closed.

The holes move with it, almost as far. A 10% error in that correction moves the foot 2.2–2.4 mm and every hole 2.0–2.4 mm. Trimming the foot puts the fundamental right but leaves the holes where the model put them, so expect to tune each one. A hole that plays flat can be opened with a file. A hole that plays sharp cannot be closed up.

The bore is 3/4in, the size for A4. Flutopedia's bore table puts A4 near 3/4in, so these sit with most flutes in this key.

The flue is a fill engrave, the full width of the bore, in two colours: red on the underside of the bottom bird layer, and magenta on the top plate over the plug. Run one for a bird or a nest flue, about 1 mm deep, or both, about 0.5 mm each, for a savanna flue. The depth is a power setting, so find it on scrap first.

File the splitting edge by hand. The window's far edge, where the air splits, needs a clean bevel of about 30°, and a laser cuts square, charred edges. File it before assembly, while the top is still a flat plate on the bench; if the edge goes wrong, recut the top.

Cut it in 3 mm Baltic birch plywood, at a 0.13 mm kerf. The finger joints are the ones on the trumpet bores in trumpet-elbows-allowed: Boxes.py's own, with 6 mm fingers and 6 mm spaces and no play, so the fit comes from the kerf compensation alone (a -flat box glues its sides instead). If your laser's kerf is not 0.13 mm, redraw the sheets for its own with --kerf=: a press-fit that comes out loose wants a wider kerf, one too tight to close a narrower one. The plug discs are drawn 0.12 mm over the bore, the mallet fit used on the lyre-harp. Press-fit, then seal every seam with shellac. The doubler, the bird's layers, every layer of the stack and every mouthpiece cap are glued.

Run the engraves first, then the cuts. Red and magenta engrave the flue's two halves, either or both; blue engraves the depressions; then orange cuts the holes, slots and windows, and black frees the parts. Red and magenta are outside the house order every LaserMadeMusic repository uses, whose one engrave colour is blue: the two halves need a colour each so that either can be left off. The parts are not numbered: the 3D view shows where each one goes. Every build also carries a greyscale image round each finger hole, the depression. It needs an image engrave that turns grey into depth, such as Grayscale mode in xTool Creative Space.

Tuning

  1. Tie the bird on over the exit hole, its front edge on the window's near edge. It is tied rather than glued, so it can be slid to voice the flute. Any thin leather strip will do; the 2017 flute's, yours/cut-files/your-flute-leather-tie-cut-files.svg, is 360 × 4.6 mm, long enough to wrap any of these bodies.
  2. With every hole closed, trim the foot until the flute sounds A4.
  3. Open hole 1 and tune C5. The holes are cut to the model's size, so if a note is flat, open its hole slightly with a file.
  4. Work up the flute in order, since each hole nudges the ones above it.

The long flutes: F4 and E4

box-square-3-F4-590 and box-square-3-E4-590 (2026-11-08) are box-square-3 made as long as the bed allows, and in whatever key that length sounds. Each is 589.5 mm overall: the 600 mm bed, less 5 mm at each end and room for a kerf of up to 0.5 mm. The six A4 builds solve the sound chamber for A4. These two hold it at the length the bed gives, and the model finds the note and lays the holes out for the minor pentatonic on it.

  • The bore is wider: a 19.70 mm square, the area of a 22.23 mm round bore. In the 16.88 mm bore a 590 mm flute's biggest hole leaves only 1.7 mm of ply beside the finger notches; here it leaves over 4 mm.
  • The hands are 31 mm apart, not 25, so hole 3 stays near 10 mm, which large fingers seal. Closer than 28 mm and hole 3 would need to be wider than the 11 mm the model allows.
  • F4 and E4 differ only in the slow air chamber. E4 takes 30 mm from it and gives them to the sound chamber, a semitone lower in the same length. The plug seals the slow air chamber off from the bore, so its length is not in the model and moves no hole against the window. It moves the hands nearer the mouth.

Distances are from the centre of the window, in mm. The holes play the minor pentatonic on the closed note: A♭4, B♭4, C5, E♭5, F5 on the F4 flute and G4, A4, B4, D5, E5 on the E4. The model puts F4 1.1 cents flat and E4 3.3 cents flat, and every note within 0.01 cents of its target. As on every build, the window is the uncalibrated part: trim the foot to pitch and tune each hole. The fingers re-count along the length: 44 a side, against 36 on box-square-3.

naf_model.rs's design_held() solves these: the closed note first, then the holes as design() lays them out, until neither moves. They are not on the 3D view.

target/release/page_assets                             # viewer.js, turn.html and the two plans

The generators are Rust since 2026-11-07, Python until then; the move changed no byte of any cut file, plan or page.

naf_model.rs is a transmission-matrix model, the standard way woodwind calculators predict pitch. It treats the bore as a lossy tube, using the section's area and its hydraulic radius, which is where a square differs from a circle. Each finger hole is Keefe's tone hole, open or closed. At the top, the window opens into the bore beside a short closed stub up to the plug.

square_flute.rs builds each of the six from what the model asks for and checks it before writing anything:

  • every note within a cent of its target
  • every part on the bed
  • every hole inside the bore
  • enough ply between neighbouring holes, and between a hole and a finger notch
  • the fingers flush with the outside faces
  • the top in one piece
  • the flue's two halves, nest and bird, lined up over the whole plug
  • in a box, no finger notch beside the exit hole or the window, which both reach the notches' floor
  • the cap's notch and the bottom plate's end notch both opening into the bore
  • the depressions clear of each other and of the plate's edges, and the doubler clear of the window
  • every part placed on a sheet

The crate borrows its SVG path writer from swept-curve's ribbon_bore.rs and each note's search from bore-tmm's src/models.rs, so it expects swept-curve, bore-tmm and lasermade-tools cloned beside this one.

.repro holds the exact command for every generated file here, and lists the 2017 originals as shipped without one.

Bird, nest or savanna flue: woodland, plains and between

Where the flue is carved is chosen at the laser, not in the file. Every sheet carries both halves, each in a colour of its own: red in the underside of the bird, and magenta in the nest, the top plate's face over the plug. The two are lined up, so run either or both:

Run Where the flue is carved Style Depth
red only the underside of the block, the bird woodland about 1 mm
magenta only the flute's body, the nest under the bird, as in the 2017 flute plains, or prairie about 1 mm
both half in each savanna, where the woodland meets the prairie: not a traditional style, an experiment about 0.5 mm each

Bird and nest are the two traditional places for a Native American flute's flue, kept side by side on purpose; savanna, half in each, is an experiment between them. Until 2026-10-18 each was a build of its own, eighteen in all.

The two put the airstream at different heights. A flue carved in the bird has the nest's flat surface as its floor, so the air leaves just above the top plate. A flue carved in the nest has the bird's flat underside as its roof, so the air leaves just below the top plate's surface. A savanna flue straddles the joint, so the air leaves at the top plate's surface itself, midway between the two. Against the splitting edge on the window's far side, that is a shift of about one flue depth, roughly 1 mm, which is one reason the two styles voice differently. The model does not simulate the jet, so which suits these flutes is for a test cut to show.

A third traditional way forms the flue with a spacer plate: a thin plate with a slot cut through it, between a flat bird and a flat nest. Its floor is the nest, so the air leaves above the top plate exactly as from a bird flue; only the making differs. The depth is the spacer's thickness rather than an engrave, so it is exact and even; the floor and roof are clean ply faces rather than a charred groove; a different depth is a different spacer, not a new bird; but it is one more surface to seal, and it wants stock about 1 mm thick, not 3 mm. None of the builds here uses one. Flutopedia's anatomy of the flute and its glossary describe all three traditional ways.

Where the flue is carved moves no hole, so the three share a hole layout, in the table above. The bird's layers and the cap are glued, as in the original. Two things are not taken. The engraved splitting edge charred, so the edge is still filed by hand. And the original's stepped block was for looks only, so the bird keeps its plain layers.

The author's flute

The builds above began as a fresh take, worked out without seeing this one. This flute was designed, cut and played in 2017, and its files are in yours/original/ exactly as they were. Measured out of yours/original/NAF_flatjoints.svg:

Bore 19.37 wide × 22.44 mm deep, 435mm²
Sound chamber 313.25 mm from the window's centre to the foot
Window 19.37 × 6.0 mm, the whole width of the bore
Plug 11 mm, its top engraved as the flue
Finger holes 7.73, 7.87, 7.79, 7.79 and 5.47 mm, 213.9, 185.7, 156.9, 109.5 and 78.0 mm from the window

It tried two things the first designs here did not:

  • An engraved splitting edge. FluteEdge.png is a greyscale ramp, black at the window and white 9.9 mm on, engraved beside the window so the laser would cut the bevel itself. It did not work, so the edge is filed by hand, as it is in every build.
  • A soft engraved depression round each finger hole, ring.png, for the finger to find. These worked, cut on a Glowforge, whose 3D engrave reads a grey image as depth. On an xTool P2S, Grayscale mode in xTool Creative Space varies the power with each pixel's brightness, so the same image should work there too. Set its depth on scrap first.

SolidCraft designs

The generator also writes every build as a parametric SolidCraft design, in solidcraft/: the rules it follows are expressions there, so changing one number rebuilds the rest.

Open a .sc in SolidCraft. The parameters are under the SOLID tab, MODIFY panel, Change Parameters; the Browser lists the parts and the timeline every step. The .solidcraft.json beside it is the script it was built from (solidcraft-cli run FILE --save NAME.sc). The .sc holds the design without SolidCraft's build cache, which it rebuilds on opening, so it is the same file every time. A STEP file in step/ is the built parts only, with no parameters:

finger · finger_end · finger_clear 6 · 12 · 3 mm a box's fingers (twice ply), how far they keep from each end, and from the exit hole and the window

The tuning cannot follow: the chamber and the holes are what the model's solver found for that bore and hole wall, and no expression redoes it. They are parameters so that a flute tuned by hand can be written back into its design; change the bore or the ply and they no longer play A4 until the model is run again. The counts of plug discs, bore layers and bird layers are fixed per design, since each is a part.

Read-only parameters show the results: the bore, the flute's total length and width pw, the window centre wc, a box's finger counts, and the generator's checks as room left, where below 0 fails: bed_room (the bed's length past the flute), hole_room (the largest hole inside the bore), gap_room (ply between the closest two holes, past 3 mm) and, on a box, notch_room (ply between a hole and a finger notch).

The depressions round the holes are not drawn.

Checked body by body against the generator's own parts, as the earlier STEP files held them: every part of all six builds matches to within 0.012 mm, the sag of those files' straight runs round a curve. The difference is the flue, which they left out. The box's fingers re-count along the flute as the generator counts them, including the ones it keeps between the exit hole and the window when the plug is long enough for one. The two long builds were checked the other way round (2026-11-08): lasermade-tools' sc-flatten drew their cut sheets from the designs' solids, and sc-compare put every part within 0.03 mm of the generator's. With ply at 3.2 mm, or with a wider bore and the holes moved, every design rebuilds with no two parts overlapping but the plug discs' press-fit.

STEP files

step/ holds each design as a STEP file, built by SolidCraft from the designs above, to open or import in a CAD program such as SolidCraft, Fusion or FreeCAD: one solid body per part, in millimetres, assembled. The holes and notches are true circles, and the flue is cut 0.5 mm into each face. .repro checks them:

target/release/square_flute --no-write --solidcraft=solidcraft
solidcraft-cli run solidcraft/square-flute-box-square-3.solidcraft.json --out step/square-flute-box-square-3.step
